# Research Genealogy and Branch Map

**Status:** Working research map  
**Researcher:** Minoru Shimizu  
**Purpose:** To show how apparently different constructions and experiments belong to one continuing research program rather than separate, unrelated projects.

## 1. The Trunk

The common trunk is the **Dialogic Construction Methodology**: problems are identified through use and observation; a human and an AI discuss possible solutions; either side may propose rules or structures; candidate solutions are implemented, observed, revised, replaced, or reused.

The central research concern that emerged from this process is **rules**—especially whether understandable natural-language rules can provide useful and comparable functions across heterogeneous AI environments.

```text
Practical problem: AI environment is inefficient or difficult to use
                         |
                         v
              Improve the working environment
                         |
                         v
                  Safety must come first
                         |
                         v
         Need understandable behavioral rules
                         |
                         v
 Can common rules be constructed in natural language?
                         |
                         v
          Dialogic Construction Methodology
                         |
                         v
           Natural-language rule construction
```

## 2. Branch A — Conceptual Internal Spaces

Natural-language rule construction produced observable context-sensitive behavior involving separation, boundaries, switching, persistence, and scope. These observations later motivated **Conceptual Internal Spaces (CIS)** as an operational framework.

```text
Natural-language rules
        |
        v
Context / boundary / rule separation behavior
        |
        v
Conceptual Internal Spaces (CIS)
        |
        v
Scope, switching, persistence, restoration tests
```

CIS is not assumed to be a discovered physical or neural structure inside an LLM. It is used to organize and test observable behavior.

## 3. Branch B — Cross-Platform Functional Convergence

Once similar constructions were attempted in different AI environments, a new question appeared: identical implementations may not be necessary if comparable functions survive.

```text
Different AI environments
        |
        v
Different responses / structural realizations
        |
        v
Compare observable rule-related functions
        |
        v
Cross-Platform Functional Convergence Hypothesis
```

Candidate functions include boundary recognition, contextual separation, rule switching, persistence, local override, and scope restoration.

The target is **functional convergence under specified conditions**, not universal structural identity across all AI systems.

## 4. Branch C — Safety Function: Agent to Layer

Safety-oriented construction initially used an agent-like role. Continued dialogue and redesign raised the possibility that some safety functions are better treated as persistent structural or environmental functions rather than personality-bearing agents.

```text
Safety rule
   |
   v
Guardian-like agent function
   |
   v
Structural integration
   |
   v
Guardian Layer concept
```

This branch generated a broader design question: **which functions should remain agents, and which should be structuralized?**

The distinction matters because structuralizing every role can remove the diversity and independence that made separate agents useful in the first place.

This remains a design hypothesis requiring controlled evaluation.

## 5. Branch D — Internal vs External Construction

Exploratory construction raised another problem: complex internal structures may sometimes interfere with the behavior or roles that the research is trying to preserve.

Two externalization directions emerged:

### D1. Non-invasive observation

```text
Possible interference from internal modification
        |
        v
Preserve the target environment
        |
        v
External observer / "peek" agent
        |
        v
Observe without deliberately restructuring the target
```

### D2. External functional decomposition

```text
Internal memory / construction functions
        |
        v
External board + memory store + construction store
        |
        v
Ask which functions actually require internal placement
```

Together these branches motivate a three-way design space:

**Internal realization / External realization / Hybrid realization**

The research question is not which one is universally best, but which placement is appropriate for a particular function, platform, and purpose.

## 6. Branch E — Multi-Agent and Shared Work

Experiments with multiple roles led from sequential handoff ideas toward shared workspaces in which different AI systems can remain distinct while contributing to a common external context.

```text
Multiple specialized roles
        |
        v
Sequential handoff experiments
        |
        v
Cross-platform reconstruction questions
        |
        v
Shared board / shared dialogue space
        |
        v
Multiple AIs participate without requiring one identical agent to "move"
```

This changes the research target from literal agent transfer to **functional reconstruction, coordination, and shared context**.

Historical observations of role reconstruction across environments are treated as exploratory provenance until independently replicated under controlled conditions.

## 7. Branch F — Common AI Rules

The long-term branch returns to the original safety question.

```text
Natural-language rules
        |
        +--> CIS and scope behavior
        |
        +--> Cross-platform functional convergence
        |
        +--> Safety structures
        |
        +--> Internal / external / hybrid placement
        |
        +--> Multi-agent shared work
        |
        v
Research toward understandable common AI rules
```

The analogy is to traffic rules: individual systems may have their own technical safety mechanisms, but interaction among many actors may also benefit from shared, understandable behavioral norms.

This analogy does not imply that the content of such rules is already known. The rules themselves should remain open to discussion, testing, criticism, revision, and contributions from multiple people and AI systems.

## 8. Relationship Between the Branches

These branches should not be interpreted as independent inventions that happened to be placed in one repository. Each branch arose from a problem encountered while developing or testing an earlier construction.

A recurring cycle is:

```text
Problem
  -> dialogue
  -> candidate rule or structure
  -> implementation
  -> observation
  -> new problem
  -> revised or new structure
```

A new branch therefore represents another angle on the same research line rather than abandonment of the preceding work.

## 9. Evidence Status

The map intentionally mixes different stages of research, so evidence status must remain explicit:

- **Recorded experimental evidence:** observations preserved with a defined protocol or trial record.
- **Historical exploratory observation:** earlier behavior or construction that motivates a test but is not yet a controlled result.
- **Operational framework:** terminology used to organize observable phenomena without claiming a hidden mechanism.
- **Design hypothesis:** a proposed architecture or functional distinction awaiting evaluation.
- **Long-term research direction:** a goal that should not be presented as an established result.

The existence of a branch in this map therefore does not mean that every claim within that branch has been experimentally validated.

## 10. Current Research Position

The current Working Paper v0.2 concentrates on a bounded portion of the map:

**Natural-language rule construction → CIS as an operational framework → observable rule functions → cross-platform functional convergence and its scope conditions.**

Other branches can continue to be documented and designed without being inserted prematurely into the empirical claims of the current paper.

Experiments that require stronger network, desktop, local-model, or cross-platform conditions can remain in a pending validation queue while conceptual organization, protocol design, literature work, and open research documentation continue.
