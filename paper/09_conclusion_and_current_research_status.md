# 9. Conclusion and Current Research Status

## 9.1 Summary of the Present Study

This working paper examined under what conditions rules constructed solely through natural language can produce functionally comparable behavior across different LLM environments. To make the question testable without making unsupported claims about hidden model internals, the paper introduced **Conceptual Internal Space (CIS)** as an operational concept for describing context-dependent rule structures defined through natural-language boundaries.

The initial pilot experiments showed that simple natural-language contextual distinctions could support rule switching, short-term persistence, and rule isolation in the tested interactions. However, explicit CIS-style region framing did not outperform an ordinary rule-list condition on the simple tasks examined. An apparent difference observed in one Rule Isolation trial did not replicate in independent fresh conversations.

These negative and non-differential results narrow the claim. The present evidence does not establish a distinct CIS mechanism, nor does it demonstrate that explicit boundary framing is necessary for simple contextual rule-following.

## 9.2 What Has Been Observed

At the current stage, the research record supports several bounded observations.

First, natural-language instructions can define contextual distinctions that produce observable rule-dependent behavior in the tested interactions. Second, state-like persistence and switching can occur across multiple turns. Third, exploratory interactions in multiple AI environments have produced some functionally similar rule-related responses despite platform-specific differences in expression and structural behavior. Fourth, simple explicit region framing has not yet demonstrated a measurable advantage over ordinary natural-language rule presentation in the pilot tasks reported here.

These observations are intentionally narrower than a claim of universal cross-platform behavior. They identify phenomena suitable for stronger testing.

## 9.3 What Remains Hypothetical

The **Cross-Platform Functional Convergence Hypothesis** remains a working hypothesis. It proposes that heterogeneous LLM environments may differ in observable realization while converging on comparable rule-related functions under semantically equivalent natural-language specifications.

The current evidence does not establish that all AI systems exhibit such convergence. It does not establish structural identity across models, a universal natural-language rule layer, or a shared hidden mechanism. It also does not establish that CIS-style framing provides functional benefits beyond ordinary instruction-following under simple conditions.

Likewise, exploratory observations concerning boundary or structural interference, agent-like role reconstruction, and internal, external, or hybrid realizations require more controlled replication before stronger conclusions can be drawn.

## 9.4 Current Research Status

The research should therefore be understood as **exploratory and hypothesis-forming, with an initial operational framework and preliminary pilot evidence**. Its contribution at this stage is not a completed universal theory of AI rule behavior. Rather, it provides a progressively testable research program that separates observable functions from claims about hidden implementation.

The central question has consequently become more precise. Instead of asking whether one identical structure exists or works across every AI system, the next stage asks:

> **Under what conditions, across which AI systems, and for which rule-related functions does cross-platform functional convergence occur or fail?**

This formulation allows positive results, failures, platform-specific exceptions, and independent replications to progressively define the scope of the hypothesis.

## 9.5 Next Research Stage

The next experimental stage should move beyond simple binary INSIDE/OUTSIDE switching. Priority tests include nested scopes, local rule modification, restoration after leaving a scope, variable-like rule shadowing, semantic paraphrases, longer conversational distances, controlled distractors, and independent cross-platform replication.

Where practical, experiments should compare semantically matched conditions and record platform, model/version information, conversation state, exact prompts, outputs, and evaluation criteria. Tests across additional commercial, open-weight, and local AI environments can expand the evidence base without implying that exhaustive testing of all AI systems is possible.

A parallel research direction concerns architectural placement. Internal, external, and hybrid realizations can be compared to determine whether comparable functions can be preserved through different structural arrangements and whether particular placements reduce interference or improve transparency, persistence, or coordination.

## 9.6 Current Conclusion

The present study supports a cautious but meaningful conclusion:

> **Natural language can be used to construct testable contextual rule structures in LLM interactions, and preliminary observations provide a basis for investigating whether comparable rule functions can recur across heterogeneous AI environments without requiring identical structural realization.**

This conclusion is deliberately provisional. The research does not end with this paper version. The working hypothesis should be revised as new experiments, failures, independent replications, model changes, and alternative explanations become available.

The objective is therefore not to freeze a final architecture, but to continue identifying which rule-related functions are reproducible, where they fail, and what those boundaries imply for the possibility of shared natural-language rule methods across diverse AI systems.
